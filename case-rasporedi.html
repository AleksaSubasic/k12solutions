01 — The problem

Fourteen venues, fourteen spreadsheets

A hospitality group ran staffing across fourteen venues in a shared spreadsheet that nobody trusted. Managers built rotas on Sunday nights, staff found out by WhatsApp screenshot, and swaps happened verbally and then vanished. Payroll spent three days a month reconstructing what had actually been worked. Off-the-shelf tools existed, but all of them priced per employee across 400 seasonal staff — the maths never worked.

02 — The approach

What we decided to do about it.

Scope

Sprints, not a deadline

Seven weeks in four sprints, each ending with something usable in one real venue. No big-bang launch, no eight-week silence.

Interface

Learnable between services

The manager view is one screen. Drag a person onto a shift. Everything else — costs, overtime warnings, coverage gaps — is feedback on that one action.

Billing

Per venue, not per head

Priced against the way hospitality actually staffs. This was a commercial decision that came out of the build, not the brief.

03 — Zero to live

The whole build, in order.

Week 1

Discovery in the venues

Two shifts observed, four managers interviewed. The rota is built around the till's busy hours, not around people — that reframed the whole interface.

Weeks 2–3

Core scheduling

Multi-tenant accounts, roles and permissions, the drag-to-assign week view, and live labour-cost totals as you build the rota.

Week 4

Staff side

Mobile view, availability, shift swaps with manager approval, push notifications. Piloted in one venue for a full week.

Weeks 5–6

Billing and reporting

Stripe subscriptions per venue, payroll export, and the reports the finance team asked for after seeing the pilot.

Week 7

Rollout

Migrated all fourteen venues, trained managers in two group sessions, and ran the old spreadsheet in parallel for a fortnight.

04 — Two calls worth explaining

Where we pushed back.

We built the cost line early

Showing live labour cost while a manager drags shifts was scheduled for sprint four. We moved it to sprint two after watching a manager finish a rota, export it, open Excel, and discover she was 900€ over. Feedback belongs at the moment of the decision.

[ rota builder with cost line — 1600×900 ]

Swaps need a human in the loop

Fully automatic shift swaps were in the brief. The pilot week showed why they are a bad idea in a kitchen: two juniors swapped onto the same Saturday and nobody senior was on. Manager approval was added, and it is a one-tap action.

01 — The problem

Nobody understood what it did

ARTINT had traction inside a Discord community and nowhere else. The founders could explain the product in a two-minute conversation, but the landing page they had was a wall of feature names — “references, workflows, progressive sketches” — that meant nothing to someone arriving cold from a link. Signups were coming almost entirely from people who had already been told about it by a friend. The site was not selling; it was confirming.

02 — The approach

What we decided to do about it.

Position

One sentence, then proof

We cut the feature list and led with the outcome an illustrator actually wants. Everything below the fold exists to make that one sentence believable — not to add more claims.

Show, don't list

The product is the screenshot

Instead of icons for abstract features, each section is a real frame from the app with one line of caption. Nine screenshots replaced about four hundred words.

Signup

No page break before the ask

The signup form lives inside the page, not behind a button on a separate route. Removing that jump was the single biggest conversion change we made.

03 — Zero to live

The whole build, in order.

Day 1

Brief and scope

Ninety minutes with both founders, recorded. We left with the one sentence, a list of nine screens worth screenshotting, and a written scope they signed the same afternoon.

Days 2–3

Structure and copy

Wireframe and the full copy deck in one document, reviewed as text before anything was designed. Two rounds, both by comment, no meetings.

Days 4–6

Design and build in parallel

Sections were designed and shipped to staging one at a time, so the founders were reacting to a live page on their phones from day four rather than a flat mockup at the end.

Day 7

Signup flow

Email capture, verification, and the first-run screen — wired to their existing backend, tested with fifteen real addresses.

Days 8–9

Performance and launch

Image pipeline, font subsetting, Lighthouse pass to 98, analytics, OG cards, DNS. Live at 16:40 on day nine.

04 — Two calls worth explaining

Where we pushed back.

We killed the hero animation

The first build had the product animating on load. It looked impressive and it pushed the first meaningful paint past two seconds on a mid-range Android — which is what half their traffic uses. A static frame with one hover state tested better and loads in under a second.

[ hero, before / after — 1600×900 ]

The pricing page came later

They wanted pricing at launch. We argued for shipping without it: the product was still free, and a pricing table would have implied a paid tier that did not exist yet. We built the section, kept it unpublished, and turned it on six weeks later when the tiers were real.
